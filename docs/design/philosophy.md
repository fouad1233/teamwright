# Joinery

*A design philosophy.*

Joinery is the art of making separate parts hold together without nails. Its
beauty is never applied afterwards. It is the fit itself: the shared edge, the
exact gap, the piece that locks the others in place. A work in this manner
shows how it is made and hides nothing, and it must look as if it came from
the bench of someone who has spent a lifetime at the craft: measured twice,
cut once, then corrected by a hair until nothing can be moved.

Form is modular and rectilinear. Everything sits on one unit and its
multiples, and parts meet along true lines with a constant reveal between
them, like the kerf a saw leaves. Corners are eased, never rounded into
softness. There is no ornament and no depth that is not earned: a hairline
rule does the work that a shadow would do elsewhere. Empty space is treated
as a material of its own, planed flat and given the largest share of the
sheet, so that the few dense passages read as deliberate concentrations of
labor.

Color comes from the drafting table. The ground is paper or graphite, warm
rather than neutral, and the drawing is ink. One metal, brass, is used the way
an instrument maker uses it: sparingly, at the single point where the parts
are held. Any further color is pigment, slightly earthen, laid down flat like
a draftsman's pencils and reserved strictly for telling things apart. Nothing
glows, nothing fades into anything else, and no hue is present that does not
carry a meaning that could be written in a legend.

Scale is a conversation between the very large and the very small. Broad,
quiet fields and bold assembled masses are annotated by marks at the edge of
legibility: ticks, registration crosses, dimension lines, numerals set in a
monospaced hand. Rhythm comes from patient repetition, the same mark made
many times with the same care, the way hatching builds a surface out of
single strokes. This is where the hours show. Every stroke is spaced by
calculation, every repetition is exact, and the eye is rewarded for coming
closer.

Composition follows the engineering sheet rather than the poster. A margin
that is respected absolutely, a field of figures, a title block in the corner
that says what little needs saying. Balance is asymmetric and resolved, the
weight of a dark assembly answered by a long measured line. Hierarchy is
established by position, weight and scale alone.

Words are rare and they are labels, never explanations: a figure number, a
unit, a name, one quiet sentence at most. Type is set small and exact in a
grotesque of even color, with a serif of some elegance allowed for a single
gesture and a monospace for anything that is counted. If a thing can be shown
by a line, it is not written. The finished work should feel like a plate from
the handbook of a discipline that does not quite exist, meticulously crafted
by a master of it, with painstaking attention in every alignment and nothing
left that could be taken away.
