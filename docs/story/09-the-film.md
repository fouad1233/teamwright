# Chapter 9: The film

*13 and 14 November 2026*

## What happened

### A man at a desk

The founder asked for an advertisement, "very detailed", made in Blender,
and described it in one breath. A person sits at a table and thinks: I
wish I could. He remembers Teamwright, opens his laptop and tells the
agents what he wants. A sensor hub is designed step by step, the
schematic and the board in three dimensions, the case coming down over
it. At the end the person is happy.

A storyboard of ten shots went back, and the answer was three words:
"Approved, build it". The work was split the way the product splits work.
One agent took the desk and the person. One took the workshop where the
device is made. One took the pictures of the program and put the film
together. The session that had handed out the work kept the timeline,
one file with dated notes that every hand had to read before touching a
frame.

### A device that could be built

The first board in the workshop looked like a board and was not one. The
founder saw it and wrote: make sure the schematic and the board are true
and make sense, a real device, not a fake.

So the device was designed before it was drawn again. An ESP32-S3 module,
a sensor for carbon dioxide, temperature and humidity, a small display on
a header with a spacer under it, a USB-C socket with the two resistors it
needs, a fuse, a regulator. Twenty-nine parts and eighteen nets, written
down with a list of what had been checked and what had not. Only then did
the schematic draw itself in gold in the film, and the tracks follow it.

### The night the computer restarted

Rendering ran into the night. Windows installed an update and restarted
the computer. Every render stopped and every agent was gone.

Nothing on disk was lost. The frames were numbered files in folders. An
agent had once removed a folder of test frames with one command, and
since then the rule was that nobody deletes a folder of frames. In the
morning the lock of a renderer that no longer existed was
removed, the missing frames were counted, and the work went on from the
number where it had stopped.

### A website that could not leave the house

The founder asked for a website too, on GitHub Pages: a page on which
scrolling moves through the film. It was built without a framework, from
one page, one style sheet and one script
([decision 0023](../decisions/0023-a-website-without-a-framework.md)).
The film's pictures do not live in the repository. They are fetched from a
release when the site is published.

The film was finished at 82 seconds and the founder wrote "Yes, publish".
Nothing could be published. The sign-in to GitHub on that computer had
gone stale, and signing in is a thing only the founder can do. The first
command that was handed over was written for the wrong shell and failed
with an error. Ten commits waited on the computer until the evening.

### "The focus should be at the middle of the screen"

A first voice was made on the computer itself. The founder listened, liked
one of the voices, and then said the thing that mattered about the
picture. When the film shows the program it is not good enough. The focus
should be in the middle of the screen. It should be very clear where to
look. Maybe enlarge things. Use smooth motion, motion that makes sense.
And: do not hand this to another agent, make it yourself.

The founder was right. That part of the film filled the frame with a whole
screen of the program and let a pointer hop from tab to tab. The eye had
nowhere to rest.

It was made again by four rules. What the voice speaks of lies in the
middle of the frame, large. One thing at a time comes forward, sharp and
bright, and everything else goes dark and soft behind it. The view
travels with moves that are eased at both ends and blur along their way.
The pointer comes only where a hand acts: three presses in forty-four
seconds.

To bring a thing forward large it has to be sharp. So the program was
photographed again at twice the sharpness: the same stored project, with
the clock of the page set back to the evening of the first pictures, and
for the moments that had passed, the page was shown the data as it had
stood then. Laid beside the first pictures, the new ones differed in less
than a hundredth of a percent. The brief was typed into the real field
letter by letter, 233 letters, a picture after each.

### A voice nobody in the room could hear

The founder then chose a voice from ElevenLabs, signed in to it through
its own tool, and asked for a direction in square brackets before each
line, the way a director writes one: quiet and thoughtful here, a sigh
there.

The session that made the film cannot hear. It checked every take by
having it transcribed. That showed whether each word was said, and when.
It also caught a direction that had been spoken instead of acted: a maker
showing a friend his own tool came back as a sentence in the film. The
direction was shortened and the scene spoken again. The same check
unmasked a sound that had been ordered as a soft rush of air and arrived
as the closing of a plastic lid.

The moments of the words then set the time of the picture. The burndown
chart comes to the middle of the frame on the word "burndown". The film
grew from 88 seconds to 107, because a thing that is shown properly takes
longer than a thing that flashes by. Music was composed in six parts, each
as long as a chapter of the film, quiet where the man wishes and fullest
where the device comes to life.

### Out into the open

The founder asked for the sign-in to be started, entered a code in the
browser, and the ten commits went up. The film at small size looked soft,
so the website got pictures as wide as the film itself and the film at its
full size for everybody.

## Why it went this way

- **A film about a real program has to show the real program.** Every
  picture of the interface is a picture of the built application on stored
  data. When a past moment was needed again, the data was shown as it had
  been, not drawn by hand.
- **The device had to be real because the claim is real.** The film says a
  person can have a sensor hub designed. A board that could not work would
  have made the film a lie in its own picture.
- **The eye follows one thing.** Films that show software well keep the
  point of interest in the same place from one moment to the next, bring
  the meant thing forward and dim the rest. The first tour did none of
  this.
- **The voice leads.** One table says when each line is said and when its
  words are shown. The picture is built from the same table, so nothing
  can drift apart when a line or a voice changes.
- **What cannot be heard must be checked another way.** Transcription
  turned sound into something that could be read and compared.

## Decisions

- The website is plain files without a framework, and the film's media
  stays out of the repository
  ([decision 0023](../decisions/0023-a-website-without-a-framework.md)).
- Nobody deletes a folder of rendered frames.
- No line about any other company is added to the film. A sentence that
  said the project was not affiliated with one was dropped at the
  founder's word.
- The key of the voice service lies in a file that no agent opens or
  prints.

## Storyboard beats

1. A man at a desk under one lamp taps a pencil on a sketch of a small
   box. A thought bubble holds the box, finished. It fades.
2. A board that only looks like a board. A hand turns it over: the back
   is blank cardboard. The hand takes a real part list and starts again.
3. Night. Rows of frames fill a shelf one by one. The lights flicker, the
   machine goes dark, comes back. The shelf is still full to the same
   place, and a hand puts the next frame beside the last.
4. Ten letters wait in a tray by a locked door. The owner of the house
   comes with the key. The letters go out together.
5. A screen crowded with small things, and an eye that darts over it.
   Then one thing steps forward into the middle and the rest goes dim.
   The eye stops.
6. A reader with covered ears holds a strip of paper on which a voice has
   been written down. One line on it does not belong to the speech. The
   reader strikes it through and the voice is asked again.

## Note of 14 November 2026, late evening

Not checked by anybody: how the film sounds. The voice, the music and the
sounds were set by numbers and by transcription. The founder listened to
two versions and asked for the music to be louder, which was done.
